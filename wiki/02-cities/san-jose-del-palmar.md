# San José del Palmar — Chocó

- **Population (DANE 2026 official):** 5,907 (DIVIPOLA 27660 — see wiki/14-context-reference.md; continuous growth 2023-2026, an earlier aggregator "population decrease" claim was resolved as an error)
- **Severity:** EPICENTER — quake located ~5-12 km from this town
- **Status:** cut off by land for an extended period, incommunicado ~30 hours post-quake; still under-resourced as of the last dated coverage found (Aug 12)

## Deaths — CONFIRMED zero, despite being the epicenter
Consistent across ~10 outlets: **zero confirmed deaths in San José del Palmar itself.** Mayor León Fabio Marín Moncada repeatedly stated this ("No hay pérdidas humanas, es un balance positivo gracias a Dios"). This is a striking contrast to Pereira/Cali (both far from the epicenter but with much higher tolls) — likely reflects low population density/rural construction patterns rather than lower shaking intensity.

## Missing
One consistently-named individual: **José Leónidas Cano, 59**, last seen in the corregimiento of La Italia. Some outlets cite "5 missing" (El Colombiano, likely a broader regional figure) but the named-individual "1 missing" figure is more specific/credible.

## Injured
Highly variable by source: "two injured" (El País Cali) up to "138 injured" (El Colombiano — likely a regional-scope figure, not municipio-specific, treat with caution).

## Damnificados / damage
Figures vary significantly by date/outlet, reflecting an evolving census the mayor explicitly said was still in progress:
| Source | Homes affected | Fully collapsed |
|---|---|---|
| Semana | 180 | — |
| El País Cali / El Tiempo (mayor's own count) | 342 | — |
| El Espectador / Publimetro | 500+ (some affectation) | 10-20 |
| RCN | 412 | — |

Population effectively 100% affected given the town's small size (~5,800-7,500 residents cited variably vs DANE's 5,907).

## Access and rescue operations — the most substantively covered aspect
- Town cut off completely by land: the single access road (toward Cartago, Valle del Cauca) suffered 15-24+ landslides depending on report/date.
- Only helicopter access was possible (~1 hour flight from Quibdó).
- Incommunicado for roughly 30 hours post-quake (no phone, no signal).
- Notable rescue: a 17-year-old, 33 weeks pregnant, went into premature labor the day of the quake; community members hand-cleared an emergency path through mud/rock to evacuate her; helicopter evacuation around Aug 12, reported stable afterward.
- By Aug 12, a humanitarian team arrived by helicopter with medical/psychological support; a single heavy-machinery operator was working to reopen roads.

## Aid / government response
- Mayor stated explicitly (Tue Aug 11 afternoon): **"No hemos recibido recursos del Gobierno Nacional ni departamental"** — no national or departmental resources received yet. A departmental delegation was expected Wed Aug 12.
- Town has **no hospital, only a basic health center** — significant vulnerability given the injuries and the pregnancy emergency.
- Explicitly requested needs: potable water, tents/temporary shelter (especially for indigenous communities), roofing/construction materials, non-perishable food, blankets/sheets, road-clearing machinery, utility restoration.
- **No named/formal aid points or shelter locations found in any source** — response was still ad hoc/emergency-stage as of the last dated coverage, not yet organized into fixed distribution points.

## Infrastructure damage
- No electricity, no water/acueducto, no cell signal — all three out for at least 3 days per the most recent (RCN) report found.
- Damage extended to the indigenous Copé resguardo's infrastructure and school.
- Water contamination from damaged pipes, compounding pre-existing El Niño-driven water rationing.
- Both the tertiary rural roads and the sole highway to Cartago were blocked.

## Nearby municipios
- Cartago (Valle del Cauca) appears only as the destination of the blocked highway, not as a jointly-affected disaster zone.
- Sipí and Tadó (sometimes grouped with San José del Palmar in SGC's aftershock-cluster naming) were NOT found named alongside it in any damage report — one source (Publimetro) explicitly named Río Iró and Nóvita instead as other Red Cross priority areas in the region.

## Additional context
San José del Palmar suffered ELN armed attacks against the police station and military base on Aug 7-8, 2026 — just before the earthquake, compounding trauma for the town (per Semana). Worth noting as a unique contextual factor for this location.

## Coverage pattern
Press attention appears to have substantially shifted to Valle del Cauca/Coffee Triangle (Cali, Pereira) and the national death toll by Aug 11-12, with San José del Palmar coverage narrowing to "human interest / forgotten epicenter" angles (the pregnant-teen rescue, "alcalde clama por ayuda," "San José del Palmar y el Chocó también son Colombia") rather than continued hard-numbers updates. Several headlines explicitly frame the town as at risk of being overlooked. Most recent dated material found: Aug 12 — no Aug 13 coverage confirmed, but this may reflect search exhaustion rather than an actual absence of newer reporting.

## Notes
- The zero-death, low-infrastructure-support combination (epicenter town, no resources received, no hospital) makes this a priority case for the app's eventual "who's being overlooked" framing, distinct from the high-death-toll red-alert cities.
