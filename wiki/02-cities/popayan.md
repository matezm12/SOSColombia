## Infrastructure damage
- Popayán (San Camilo neighborhood): patio walls of at least one/two residences collapsed
- Santo Domingo church (Popayán) damaged; Hogar San Vicente de Paúl chapel roof collapsed
- Caldono and Morales (Cauca, not Popayán proper): church domes collapsed
- Schools damaged: Manuel de Valverde, Las Sabanas, Chamón and Chamoncito sedes, Santa Rosa classrooms
- Several downtown Popayán soportales/arcades closed to public due to facade/wall collapse risk
- Several health facilities in Popayán reported damage pending structural inspection (not named in sources found)
- ~76,000 electricity users lost power across Cauca/Popayán; restored gradually (El País, mapadelterremoto.com)
- **Guillermo León Valencia airport (Popayán) suspended operations — but NOT from earthquake structural damage.** Cause: ash/gas emissions from Volcán Puracé (orange alert since 2026-08-01; SGC recorded 3 ash columns up to 300m above crater within 24h of the quake, elevated SO2/CO2). Authorities (SGC/Aerocivil/UNGRD) explicitly stated the earthquake and the volcanic activity are independent geological processes — do not conflate the two in reporting. Puracé's ash/gas fallout also damaged crops, pastures and water sources in rural Puracé municipio (agricultural/livestock impact, separate from Popayán urban damage).

## 1983 vs 2026 comparison
The 1983 Popayán earthquake (M5.5, already documented in wiki/14-context-reference.md as ~250 dead, ~1,500 injured, 4,964 destroyed + 13,796 severely damaged structures) is a **historically separate event** from the 2026 M7.4 quake (epicenter San José del Palmar, Chocó, ~250km away) — do not merge figures between the two.

No source found this pass draws a direct comparison between the two events specifically for Popayán. The one 1983-themed piece located, El Nuevo Liberal's "¡Del terremoto de Popayán a Mocoa!" (published 2024-12-06, i.e. **before** the 2026 quake — not a retrospective written in response to it), gives a first-person 1983 account (author cites "over 300 dead, 10,000 homeless" — a modestly higher death figure than the wiki's existing ~250, worth flagging as a minor secondary-source discrepancy) and compares 1983 to the Mocoa 2017 disaster, not to any 2026 event. The absence of a 1983-vs-2026 comparison piece is itself informative: it's consistent with Popayán having been spared serious 2026 damage — nobody has had cause to write "it wasn't as bad as '83" yet.
